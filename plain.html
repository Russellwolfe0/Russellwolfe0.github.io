Research

I work in the Paton Lab (Prof. Robert Paton), a computational chemistry group. Predicting how molecules react normally takes hours of quantum-mechanical calculation per molecule. My work tests whether AI models can give the same answers in a fraction of a second, and where they fail.

TRIP50 benchmarking: can ML potentials handle triplet reactivity?

Ongoing. Python, ASE, ORCA, RDKit, AQME. Results and code are public.

TRIP50 (Hughes, Popescu & Paton, J. Chem. Theory Comput. 2026) is a set of 50 reactions that pass through a triplet state, a short-lived, high-energy form of a molecule that is hard to observe experimentally, so chemists rely on calculations to understand it. Each reaction comes with reference energies from DLPNO-CCSD(T)/CBS, a slow, very accurate quantum method. I extended the benchmark to ask a practical question: how much accuracy do you give up by replacing DFT, the standard quantum method, with a machine-learned interatomic potential (MLIP), an AI model trained to predict a molecule’s energy, and what do you get back? Across 24 methods, the best MLIP came close to the best DFT while running about 45,000 times faster.

The comparison covered 24 methods (7 DFT, 16 MLIPs and g-xTB) on 156 reference structures. The best MLIP (eSEN-MD) has a combined error of 1.8 kcal/mol, against 1.3 for the best DFT method. Lower is better.

I wrote the pipeline that runs every model on the reference molecules, parses the output files, and computes each reaction’s energy change and the energy barrier it has to climb in each direction. I report errors overall and by reaction type (C–C, C–O, C–S, C–Hal, Si–X, N–X and hydrogen-atom transfer), because model rankings change across categories. Only models that can handle the electron spin of these molecules were usable; the rest were excluded.

Follow-up: do ML potentials find the right conformer? For each of the 106 non-TS species I generated RDKit conformers with AQME (553 in total), optimized them with each of the 16 MLIPs, and checked whether the lowest-energy conformer matches the model’s own optimized reference geometry (within 0.5 kcal/mol). Match rates run from 49% to 74%. Selected disagreements were checked with ωB97M-V/def2-QZVPP single points: some lower conformers are real, others are model errors.

Other projects

Black bear populations under drought

In progress. Python, stochastic simulation, age-structured models.

How long a drought can a black bear population absorb before recovery becomes unlikely? I’m building an age-structured model with yearly births, cub and adult survival, and density dependence, then letting drought reduce breeding and cub survival. I’m checking a stochastic simulation against a matrix model built from the same rates.
