Research

I work in the Paton Lab (Prof. Robert Paton), where I test how well fast machine learning interatomic potentials (MLIPs) reproduce the energetics of open-shell organic reactions, a regime that is hard for both ML models and standard DFT.

TRIP50 benchmarking: can ML potentials handle triplet reactivity?

Ongoing. Python, ASE, ORCA, RDKit, AQME. Results and code are public.

Triplet excited-state intermediates are short-lived, so mechanisms involving them depend heavily on computation. The TRIP50 dataset (Hughes, Popescu & Paton, J. Chem. Theory Comput. 2026) gives 50 triplet-state organic reactions with DLPNO-CCSD(T)/CBS reference energies. I extended it to ask a practical question: how much accuracy do you give up by replacing DFT with an ML potential, and what do you get back?

  • 24methods: 7 DFT, 16 MLIPs, g-xTB
  • 50reactions, 156 reference structures
  • 1.8kcal/mol, best MLIP (eSEN-MD) vs 1.3 for the best DFT
  • ~45,000×faster than that DFT method

I wrote the pipeline that runs every model on the reference geometries, parses the DFT output, and computes reaction energies and forward and reverse barriers. I report errors overall and by reaction type (C–C, C–O, C–S, C–Hal, Si–X, N–X and hydrogen-atom transfer), because model rankings change across categories. Only molecular models that take spin as input were usable. Spin-unaware and materials models were excluded.

Follow-up: do ML potentials find the right conformer?

For each of the 106 non-TS species I generated RDKit conformers with AQME (553 in total), optimized them with each of the 16 MLIPs, and checked whether the lowest-energy conformer matches the model’s own optimized reference geometry (within 0.5 kcal/mol). Match rates run from 49% to 74%. Selected disagreements were checked with ωB97M-V/def2-QZVPP single points: some lower conformers are real, others are model errors.

Other projects

Black bear populations under drought

In progress. Python, stochastic simulation, age-structured models.

How long a drought can a black bear population absorb before recovery becomes unlikely? I’m building an age-structured model with yearly births, cub and adult survival, and density dependence, then letting drought reduce breeding and cub survival. I’m checking a stochastic simulation against a matrix model built from the same rates.
