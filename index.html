TRIP50: a benchmark for triplet-state reactions

Published. J. Chem. Theory Comput. 2026 (Hughes, Popescu & Paton). I’m a co-author.

TRIP50 is a dataset of 50 triplet-state organic reactions with high-level DFT reference data. It was built to study a quiet failure in DFT: SCF calculations on triplet states often converge to a spurious T2 solution instead of the correct state, and nothing in a standard workflow flags it.

The paper shows how often this happens and gives practical checks for finding and fixing it. Calculations ran on SDSC’s Expanse supercomputer through NSF ACCESS.

Machine learning potentials on triplet chemistry

Ongoing. Python, FAIRChem, ASE.

I use the FAIR Chemistry team’s tooling and universal ML potentials (UMA models) for atomistic simulations, and I evaluate how well these models and standard DFT methods handle TRIP50. Open-shell reactivity is hard for both, so it’s a useful test of where ML potentials hold up and where they break.

Other projects

Black bear populations under drought

In progress. Python, stochastic simulation, age-structured models.

How long a drought can a black bear population absorb before recovery becomes unlikely? I’m building an age-structured model with yearly births, cub and adult survival, and density dependence, then letting drought reduce breeding and cub survival. I’m checking a stochastic simulation against a matrix model built from the same rates.
